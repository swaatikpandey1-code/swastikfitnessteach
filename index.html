Chapter 1: Chemical Reactions and Equations (Quick Revision)

A chemical reaction is a process in which one or more substances change into new substances. A chemical equation represents a reaction using symbols and formulas.

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

Important Types of Reactions

  • Combination Reaction
  • Decomposition Reaction
  • Displacement Reaction
  • Double Displacement Reaction
  • Redox Reaction
Exam Tip: Always balance equations carefully.

Chapter 2: Acids, Bases and Salts

Introduction

Many substances around us are acidic or basic in nature. For example, lemon juice contains acid, while soap solution is basic. These substances show different chemical properties.

Acids

Acids are substances that produce hydrogen ions (H⁺) in aqueous solution. They are sour in taste.

Examples: Hydrochloric acid (HCl) Sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄) Nitric acid (HNO₃) Acetic acid (CH₃COOH)

Properties of Acids

  • Sour in taste
  • Turn blue litmus red
  • React with metals to produce hydrogen gas
  • React with carbonates to produce carbon dioxide
Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂

Bases

Bases are substances that produce hydroxide ions (OH⁻) in aqueous solution. They are bitter in taste and feel soapy.

Examples: Sodium hydroxide (NaOH) Potassium hydroxide (KOH) Calcium hydroxide [Ca(OH)₂]

Properties of Bases

  • Bitter in taste
  • Turn red litmus blue
  • Feel slippery

Indicators

Indicators are substances that change color in acidic or basic medium.

Litmus: Acid → Red Base → Blue Phenolphthalein: Acid → Colorless Base → Pink Methyl Orange: Acid → Red Base → Yellow

Neutralisation Reaction

The reaction between an acid and a base to form salt and water is called neutralisation.

HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

What are Salts?

Salts are ionic compounds formed during neutralisation reactions.

Important Salts

1. Common Salt (NaCl)

Used in food and is the raw material for many chemicals.

2. Baking Soda (NaHCO₃)

Used in baking, fire extinguishers and antacids.

NaCl + NH₃ + CO₂ + H₂O → NaHCO₃ + NH₄Cl

3. Washing Soda (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O)

Used in glass, soap and paper industries.

4. Plaster of Paris (CaSO₄·½H₂O)

Used for making statues, toys and casts for fractured bones.

CaSO₄·2H₂O → CaSO₄·½H₂O + 1½H₂O

pH Scale

The pH scale measures how acidic or basic a substance is. It ranges from 0 to 14.

  • pH less than 7 → Acidic
  • pH equal to 7 → Neutral
  • pH more than 7 → Basic

Importance of pH in Everyday Life

  • Digestion in stomach involves hydrochloric acid.
  • Tooth decay starts when mouth pH falls below 5.5.
  • Soil pH affects crop growth.
Topper Tip: Learn at least one equation each for acid + metal, acid + base, and preparation of Plaster of Paris.

  • Write two properties of acids.
  • What is pH scale?
